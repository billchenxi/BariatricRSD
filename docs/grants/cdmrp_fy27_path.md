## Blocker 1 — PI eligibility (the hard one)

From the FY26 PRMRP Impact Award program announcement, §2.1.2:

> "Independent investigators (e.g., Assistant Professor, Senior Scientist,
> Principal Scientist, Research Director, or equivalent) may be named by the
> organization as the Principal Investigator (PI) on the application…
> **Postdoctoral and clinical fellows and other mentored scientists are not
> eligible** to be named as an Initiating or Partnering PI."

A PhD student cannot be PI. This language is standard across CDMRP research
mechanisms. The application must be submitted by UCSC with a faculty member as
PI; you would be named key personnel.

The Partnering PI option requires one partner to be a **clinician investigator
(M.D., M.D./Ph.D., D.O.) with clinical duties**. Jeremy Balch at UF is a
plausible fit for that slot — worth confirming his degree and clinical
appointment, because it converts a weakness into a structural advantage.

## Blocker 2 — Topic-area gating

PRMRP is the largest program ($370M, 52 topic areas) but is congressionally
gated:

> "All applications for FY26 PRMRP funding must specifically address one of the
> FY26 PRMRP topic areas as directed by the U.S. Congress and have direct
> relevance to military health… If the proposed research does not specifically
> address one FY26 PRMRP topic area and one FY26 PRMRP strategic goal, then the
> government reserves the right to administratively withdraw the application."

None of the 52 FY26 topic areas covers surgery, surgical AI, obesity, bariatrics,
perioperative care, or clinical decision support. The list is dominated by rare
diseases, exposures, and specific conditions. PRMRP is therefore not a target
unless a future year's list adds a relevant topic — the list changes annually
with appropriations, so it is worth re-checking each spring.

## Blocker 3 — Military relevance is scored, not asserted

Military relevance is a required, separately reviewed component, and reviewers
are military health stakeholders. "Surgery happens in military hospitals too" is
not sufficient. A competitive application needs a military treatment facility
(MTF) or VA collaborator and a stated military health problem.

### 2. Joint Warfighter Medical Research Program (JWMRP) — Military Medical R&D Award

## The reframe that makes this fundable

Do not pitch remaining-surgery-duration prediction for bariatric surgery. Pitch
the military problem your methods actually address:

**Surgical readiness through operative workflow measurement.** DoD has a
documented concern that military surgeons lose operative case volume between
deployments and that readiness is hard to measure objectively. Your work
measures procedural workflow variation across surgeons and centers, which is
precisely a quantitative handle on that problem. Two things you already have
map onto it directly:

- The cross-center analysis (Bern → Strasbourg) is a generalization-across-
  institutions result. In DoD terms that is whether a model trained at one MTF
  transfers to another, or to a forward surgical team.
- The workflow-variability finding — conditioning helps where procedures vary,
  not where they are standardized — is an argument about *when* automated
  assessment is informative. That is directly relevant to whether workflow
  metrics can be used for readiness assessment at all.

Be honest in the application about the Phase 0 negative findings. CDMRP reviewers
respond well to investigators who state limits; the fold-variance and
cluster-instability results demonstrate you will not oversell.

## Nine-month preparation plan (Oct 2026 – Jun 2027)

1. **Secure a faculty PI at UCSC.** Non-negotiable. Have the conversation now,
   not in spring.
2. **Confirm the clinician partner.** Balch's degree and clinical appointment
   determine whether the Partnering PI option is available.
3. **Find an MTF or VA collaborator.** This is the long pole and the thing most
   likely to fail. Start with the surgical services at a nearby military
   facility or the VA Palo Alto system. Without this the military relevance
   score sinks the application regardless of the science.
4. **Register early.** The organization must be registered in SAM.gov and
   Grants.gov, and the PI in eBRAP. UCSC's sponsored research office will have
   SAM/Grants.gov handled; eBRAP registration is the PI's responsibility and
   takes longer than people expect.
5. **Subscribe to eBRAP email notifications** for CRRP, JWMRP, PRMRP and ORP so
   pre-announcements arrive automatically. Also monitor Grants.gov under
   assistance listing number **12.420**.
6. **Publish the NeurIPS paper.** Preliminary data is required for translational
   mechanisms, and a peer-reviewed publication is far stronger than a preprint.
7. **Re-check the FY27 PRMRP topic area list** when appropriations pass. If a
   relevant topic appears, PRMRP's $370M dwarfs the alternatives.

## Honest alternative: CDMRP may be the wrong funder

CDMRP funds disease- and condition-specific medical research with military
relevance. This project's contribution is methodological — evaluation protocol
design, generalization under distribution shift, when conditioning information
helps. That is a better match for:

- **NIH F31** (predoctoral fellowship) — you can be the fellow, unlike CDMRP,
  where you cannot be PI. Directly addresses the eligibility blocker.
- **NIBIB R21** with a clinical collaborator as PI — surgical data science is
  squarely in scope.
- **AHRQ** — health systems, perioperative efficiency, patient safety.
- **NSF Smart Health (SCH)** — joint NSF/NIH, explicitly for ML methods with
  clinical translation; methodological novelty is an asset there rather than a
  liability.

The NSF SCH and NIH F31 routes fit the work as it actually exists. CDMRP fits
only after the readiness reframe and a military partner. Pursue CDMRP if you
want the military application; pursue the others if you want to fund the science
you are already doing.
